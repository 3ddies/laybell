Join a live studio session from your computer — pro mics and interfaces, FL Studio / BandLab audio, and one-tap vocal polish.

Before you join. Use Chrome or Edge on Windows — sending your DAW's audio needs desktop capture, which Safari and Firefox don't do. And if your DAW is on ASIO, switch it to a Windows/WASAPI driver first (FL Studio: Options → Audio Settings → Device). ASIO bypasses the Windows mixer, so the share goes through but carries silence.
In sessionconnected

  1. Pick your mic. Choose your interface above. On Raw input the signal reaches the room untouched — right for interfaces, instruments and anything already mixed.
  2. Send your beat. Hit Share app audio, choose the Window tab, pick your DAW, and tick “Share audio.”
    It shared but nobody can hear it

    Three causes, in order of likelihood. The “Share audio” checkbox is off by default and easy to miss. You picked Entire screen — Chrome only offers that checkbox for a window or a browser tab. Or your DAW is still on ASIO, which never reaches the Windows mixer.

  3. Record their take into your DAW. This direction needs one free tool — Windows won't hand a browser's audio to a DAW on its own.
    Set up the return path

    1. Install VB-CABLE (free) and reboot — it adds a virtual audio device.
    2. Windows Settings → System → Sound → Volume mixer → set this browser's output to CABLE Input. The room now feeds the cable.
    3. You just made the browser silent on your speakers. Sound Control Panel → Recording → CABLE Output → Properties → Listen → tick “Listen to this device” and pick your headphones.
    4. In your DAW, set a track's input to CABLE Output, arm it, record.

    Leave that track's monitoring OFF. Your DAW output is going back into the room — if it also plays the incoming vocal, the artist hears themselves echoed and it can build into a loop.

  4. Sync the take. When the host fires a count-in, this page counts 4 beats then flashes REC. Punch record on the flash.
    Why the take lands slightly late

    The count-in aligns when you both start, but the audio still travels phone → room → your machine. Expect the recording to sit a little behind the grid. Track a clap on the first take to measure your offset, then nudge every take back by that amount. This is network latency, not a setting — for a sample-tight vocal, have the artist record locally and send you the file.
