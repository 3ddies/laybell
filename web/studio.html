Laybell Studio

Join a live studio session from your computer — pro mics and interfaces, FL Studio / BandLab audio, and one-tap vocal polish.

Before you join. Use Chrome or Edge on Windows — sending your DAW's audio needs desktop capture, which Safari and Firefox don't do. And if your DAW is on ASIO, switch it to a Windows/WASAPI driver first (FL Studio: Options → Audio Settings → Device). ASIO bypasses the Windows mixer, so the share goes through but carries silence.
In sessionconnected

  1. Pick your mic. Choose your interface above. On Raw input the signal reaches the room untouched — right for interfaces, instruments and anything already mixed.
  2. Send your beat. Hit Share app audio, choose the Window tab, pick your DAW, and tick “Share audio.”
    It shared but nobody can hear it

    Three causes, in order of likelihood. The “Share audio” checkbox is off by default and easy to miss. You picked Entire screen — Chrome only offers that checkbox for a window or a browser tab. Or your driver is taking the sound card exclusively, so nothing reaches Windows for Chrome to capture.

    Not all ASIO is equal here. A hardware ASIO driver or ASIO4ALL usually grabs the device outright and the share carries silence. FL Studio ASIO shares the device with other apps, so it works fine — if the room can hear your beat, your driver is not the problem and you can stop looking here.

  3. Hear them — nothing to install. Leave your DAW's output and this browser's output BOTH on your headphones. Your beat and their vocal mix in your ears, and you are done: no extra software, no routing, and the artist can never hear themselves.

    Do not send the room back into your DAW unless you follow the next step exactly. Chrome shares your DAW's whole master, so the moment their vocal is playing inside your DAW it is also being sent straight back to them — they hear themselves a beat late, and it can build into a howl. This is the single most common way to break a session.

  4. Optional — record their take into your DAW. Only if you want the vocal on a track while you work. Windows cannot hand a browser's audio to a DAW on its own, so this one needs a free driver. Most sessions do not need it: see “a cleaner way to get the take” below.
    Part 1 — install VB-CABLE (about 3 minutes)

    Download — vb-audio.com/Cable, the Windows download. Free. You get a .zip.

    Unzip — right-click it → Extract All.

    Install — in that folder, right-click VBCABLE_Setup_x64.exe → Run as administrator → Install Driver.

    Reboot. Nothing works until you do.

    You now have two new audio devices in Windows, and they are two ends of one pipe:
    CABLE Input = a speaker. Audio you send INTO the pipe.
    CABLE Output = a microphone. Audio coming OUT of the pipe.

    Part 2 — send Laybell into the pipe

    Windows Settings → System → Sound → Volume mixer → find this browser in the app list → set its Output to CABLE Input.

    Laybell now plays into the pipe instead of your headphones — so you have gone deaf to the room. The next bit gets your ears back, and it needs Windows' old sound window, because the modern Settings app cannot do it.

    How to open the old sound window. Press Win+R, type mmsys.cpl, press Enter. That is the whole trick.
    Prefer clicking? Settings → System → Sound → scroll to the bottom → More sound settings. On Windows 10 it is called Sound Control Panel instead.
    You are in the right place when you see a small window with Playback / Recording / Sounds / Communications tabs. Everything below happens in there.

    In that window: Recording tab → click CABLE Output → Properties → Listen tab → tick “Listen to this device” → under Playback through this device choose your headphones → Apply.

    Part 3 — get the pipe into your FL Studio mixer

    “My mixer only shows in 1 and in 2.” That list is not everything on your PC — it is the inputs of the one device FL is using. Where you change that device depends on your driver, and the two work completely differently. Check Options → Audio Settings to see which you are on.

    1a. On ASIO (FL Studio ASIO, ASIO4ALL, your interface's own driver) — there is only ONE Device box, for input and output together. The input lives inside the driver instead: click “Show ASIO panel”, just under Buffer length, and set the input there to CABLE Output.
    Then expect the mixer to still say “in 1 / in 2” — ASIO names channels, not devices, so CABLE Output will never appear there by name. Those two ARE the cable now. Your Audio Settings status line confirms it: “1x2 inputs available”.

    1b. On WASAPI — there is a separate Input dropdown under the output one. Set it to CABLE Output, and it then appears in the mixer BY NAME.

    2. Open the Mixer and click an empty insert track.

    3. At the top of the mixer, open the IN dropdown and pick your input — in 1 / in 2 on ASIO, or CABLE Output by name on WASAPI.

    4. Check the meter before anything else. Get the artist to talk and watch that insert's peak meter. Moving = FL has the signal. Dead = it does not, and no amount of pressing record will help.

    Careful — you can hear them without FL receiving anything. “Listen to this device” from Part 2 sends the artist to your headphones through WINDOWS, going around FL completely. So it sounds exactly the same whether FL is wired up or not. The meter is the only honest test. If you can hear them but the meter is dead, step 1 above did not take.

    Then mute that track's send to Master. Chrome shares your master, so anything sitting in it is being sent back to the artist. Click the little arrow between that insert and Master to switch the send off. You will still hear the artist — that comes from “Listen to this device” in Part 2, not from FL.

    FL can only use ONE input device at a time on WASAPI. So while its input is CABLE Output, your studio mic is not available inside FL. That is fine — choose the mic in the Microphone / audio interface box at the top of this page instead. It goes straight to the room, and the beat still reaches the room through Share app audio. Both are sent at once.

    Part 4 — actually lay it down on the beat

    Signal reaching the mixer is not the same as FL recording it. Hearing them and capturing them are two separate things, and this is where most people get stuck.

    1. Song mode, not Pattern mode. The switch is by the transport at the top. In Pattern mode the take goes into a pattern instead of onto the timeline next to your beat.

    2. ARM the insert. On that mixer insert, click the small record-arm circle at the bottom of the channel strip so it lights up. FL records armed inserts and nothing else — this is the step that is missed most often, and it fails completely silently.

    3. Set where the file goes if FL asks. It will prompt for a name and folder the first time.

    4. Click the transport Record button, then Play. If FL asks what to record, choose “Audio, into the playlist”. Picking Automation or Score records no audio at all and gives you no warning.

    5. Stop. The take appears in the playlist as an audio clip, on the beat.

    It will sit slightly behind the grid. The vocal travelled phone → room → your machine, and that delay is baked into the file — it is physics, not a setting. Record a clap on the first take, measure how late it lands, then drag every take back by that amount. If the timing has to be perfect, use “a cleaner way to get the take” below instead.

    Part 5 — fix crackling, glitching or stuttering

    It is almost never the microphone. It is a sample-rate mismatch, and the cable defaults to 44100 while this app runs at 48000.

    Set 48000 Hz in all four of these — they are configured separately. FL's is right at the top of Options → Audio Settings, and a fresh install is usually on 44100:

    · your interface's own control panel
    · FL Studio → Options → Audio Settings
    · the old sound window (Win+R → mmsys.cpl) → Playback tab → CABLE Input → Properties → Advanced
    · the same window → Recording tab → CABLE Output → Properties → Advanced

    Still crackling? Raise FL's buffer length in Audio Settings.

    A cleaner way to get the take

    A vocal pulled back through the room has network delay baked into it and will never sit tight to the grid, no matter how carefully you nudge it. For anything you intend to keep, have the artist record on their phone and send you the file — it arrives clean, dry and perfectly in time.

  5. Sync the take. When the host fires a count-in, this page counts 4 beats then flashes REC. Punch record on the flash.
    Why the take lands slightly late

    The count-in aligns when you both start, but the audio still travels phone → room → your machine. Expect the recording to sit a little behind the grid. Track a clap on the first take to measure your offset, then nudge every take back by that amount. This is network latency, not a setting — for a sample-tight vocal, have the artist record locally and send you the file.
