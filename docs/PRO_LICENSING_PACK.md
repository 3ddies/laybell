# PRO licensing pack — BMI & ASCAP

Everything needed to apply for performance-rights licences, in one place. Keep the
answers consistent across both applications: they compare notes, and a service
described two different ways invites questions you don't want.

---

## Why both, and why now

A songwriter affiliates with **exactly one** PRO. ASCAP and BMI represent
different, non-overlapping rosters. Licensing only one leaves you performing the
other's writers without rights — on roughly half your catalogue.

You need these even though every track is an original indie upload. An affiliated
songwriter has **already assigned** their performance right to their PRO, so they
cannot grant it to you in your Terms of Service no matter how the clause is
drafted. This is the single most-missed point by music-app founders.

Applications take **weeks to months** for a service type PROs haven't seen before.
This is the longest lead time on the whole launch checklist, so it sets the
earliest possible launch date.

If you genuinely don't know, say "pre-launch, no usage data yet; happy to report
actuals monthly once live."

## THE QUESTION TO ASK BOTH, IN WRITING

This is the whole reason to do this by email rather than just submitting a form:

> Does this licence cover **on-demand interactive streaming** *and* **livestream
> audio-visual transmission**?

A standard "website and mobile app" audio licence may cover on-demand streaming but
**not** livestreaming, because a livestream combines music with moving images.
Discovering that after you've launched a live product is the expensive way. **Save
the written reply.**

## Step 2 — BMI (online form, then email)

BMI's route is a web application rather than an email address:
**<https://www.bmi.com/digital_licensing/apply>**

Background on their digital licensing:
<https://www.bmi.com/digital_licensing/digital_licensing>

Their published floor for a digital music licence is **from $350/year**, typically
a one-year term with quarterly reporting based on aggregate stream hours.

**Fill the form using the fact sheet above.** Where it offers a free-text
description, paste the service description verbatim.

⚠️ **Revenue-definition gotcha:** BMI's gross revenue definition explicitly
includes *"revenues from advertising, sponsorship, subscriptions, transactional
purchases, donations, commissions from third party transactions."* Your **tips**
and your **15% shop commission** are inside the royalty base. Don't omit them.

### Answers used on the form (2026-07-28)

| Field | Answer | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Are you a songwriter? | **No** | You're licensing, not affiliating |
| Licence for content on social media (YouTube/IG/Twitch)? | **No** | Laybell **is** the platform, not a creator posting to one |
| Music Type | **Audio Only** | ⚠️ Form allows only one — see below |
| Music Use | **On Demand Music Service (User Controlled Format)** | "User controlled" = interactive, the legally significant category. NOT "Limited Music Use", which is for incidental background music |
| Primary genre | Hip-Hop / Rap | |
| Third-party platforms | *(none selected)* | That list is about distributing through Amazon/Apple TV/Roku; Laybell is a standalone app |

⚠️ **The Music Type gap.** The form forces a choice between "Audio Only" and
"Audio-Visual Only" and won't accept both. Audio Only is correct for the core
service, but Laybell **also** has audio-visual uses — music attached to short-form
video, and live audio-video broadcasts. The form cannot express this, so it must be
raised with a human. That's what the message below is for. **Send it; don't wait to
be asked.**

### ✅ OUTCOME (2026-07-28): approved on the spot

BMI offered the **Digital Multi-Use Performance License Agreement**:

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Fee | **$385**, one-time |
| Term | **One year** — renews annually |
| Reporting | **None required** |
| Grants | Streaming performance rights for the **BMI repertoire** |

Two consequences worth recording:

- **No reporting requirement removes a build task.** The quarterly stream-hour
  reporting pipeline flagged elsewhere in this doc is not needed for BMI. (The MLC
  reporting obligation is separate and still applies if that route is taken.)
- **The one-year term is now a recurring deadline.** If it lapses, Laybell is
  performing BMI repertoire unlicensed. Diary it alongside the DMCA agent renewal
  and the Maryland SDAT filing.

### Reading of the actual agreement (2026-07-28)

**The audio-visual question is resolved — no email needed for it.** §3.A grants
public performance of BMI Works "as part of LICENSEE's **Service**", and §2.F
defines Service by *delivery channel* (web site, applications, embedded player),
not by audio versus video. The Service Summary described livestreaming. The "Audio
Only" dropdown was a **rate-tier categorisation, not a limit on the grant**.

**What this licence does NOT cover (§3.B)** — public performance of *compositions*
only. Explicitly excluded:

| Not covered | Which Laybell feature | Where the right actually comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Sound recordings | All playback | The ToS grant from uploaders |
| Reproduction / distribution | **Offline downloads** | Mechanical — the MLC |
| Synchronisation | **"Use this sound"**, video posts | The ToS grant. No PRO licence ever covers sync |

None of this is a problem — but don't mistake the licence for broader cover.

### 🚨 §8 — the licence auto-terminates

| Tier | Fee | Max gross revenue | Max aggregate stream hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 (current)** | **$385** | **$18,500** | **59,000** |
| 2 | $770 | $37,000 | 118,000 |
| 3 | $1,540 | $74,000 | 236,000 |

Exceed **either** ceiling by 20% — **$22,200** or **70,800 hours** — and the
agreement "shall automatically terminate." No invoice, no warning, no notice. Every
performance afterwards is unlicensed. §8 also imposes an affirmative duty to inform
BMI *before* crossing.

were committed 2026-07-28 but left out of every run bundle; they were applied via

**§1 — no automatic renewal.** The term simply runs Start Date → End Date and
stops. The annual reminder must say **"re-apply to BMI"**, not "pay the invoice."

**§9 — reporting is optional for BMI**, *unless* usage data is given to another
performance-rights licensor. If ASCAP requires reports, BMI must receive the same.

**§11** — Laybell warrants its estimates are good-faith and that actuals are not
expected to materially exceed them. Another reason the meter matters.

### 🔴 BMI REPLIED 2026-07-29 — and the premise of the question is now stale

BMI answered §1 and ducked §2(E)(iii):

- **§1 confirmed.** No automatic renewal. They contact you before the 12-month term
  ends to ask whether you still need the licence and to update annual gross revenue
  totals (which can move you up a tier). A new agreement each year, handled through
  their online Licensing Center. Our reading was right.
- **Gross Revenue: not answered.** Instead they asked back: *"If you are not
  processing the payment, how do you generate the commission? Is the transaction
  initiated on Laybell? Do you have a beta link?"*

They asked because **the description they were given no longer matches the app.**
The email (2026-07-28) described the VENUE model — "Laybell is not the seller and
does not process the payment." `shop_credits.sql` landed on the live database
2026-07-29 and replaced it: buyers purchase credits through Apple/Google IAP, spend
them in the Shop, Laybell debits its own ledger, retains `shop_fee_rate()` (0.30) and
credits the seller 70%, withdrawable through Stripe Connect. **Laybell is in the
payment flow now.**

That actually makes the question answerable, but it must be re-asked against the new
facts. Three candidates, and the gap between them is the whole tier decision:

1. gross face value of credits users purchase,
2. what Laybell receives from Apple/Google after their 30%, or
3. what Laybell retains after creator payouts (~$0 at Apple 30% + a 30% shop fee).

⚠️ **`lib/legal/marketplace.json` §5 still describes the old model** ("you send a
request, arrange payment with the Seller"). The 2026-07-29 payment-copy sweep hit
Terms, Advertiser Terms and Community Guidelines and missed this file. It is
published at `laybell.app/marketplace.html` — fix it BEFORE sending BMI any link, or
Laybell's own terms contradict what it is telling its licensor.

### The one email actually worth sending

§2(E)(iii) counts "commissions, referral fees and any revenue generated from third
parties on all transactions initiated through the Service." For the shop, that is
genuinely ambiguous — and given §8, it decides the tier.

If users trade $100,000 and Laybell keeps 15%: is Gross Revenue **$15,000**
(comfortably Tier 1) or **$100,000** (auto-terminated)? Signing is fine either way;
the answer affects tier selection at renewal.

## Step 3 — SESAC and GMR: a documented decision to defer

Both are invite-only, publish no rates, and require direct negotiation. Together
they're a small but non-zero slice of US repertoire.

Deferring is defensible. Deferring *by accident* is not. Write the decision down —
a dated note saying you assessed SESAC and GMR, concluded the exposure was small
relative to cost at launch scale, and will revisit at a stated trigger (e.g. first
$50k of revenue, or first takedown notice naming either).

---

## Step 4 — the MLC decision (needs counsel first)

**Do not file anything here until an attorney has weighed in.** You have two paths
under §115, and picking wrong is expensive:

- **Take the blanket licence** — pay the annual administrative assessment
  (**$2,500/yr** under 10,000 monthly unique recordings, **$5,000** at 10–25k,
  **$60,000** above 25k) and file monthly usage reports.
- **Rely on voluntary licences** from your uploaders under §115(d)(1)(C) — but then
  determine whether you must still file a Notice of Nonblanket Activity. Getting
  that wrong exposes you to **treble damages plus attorney's fees**.

⚠️ **Model the 25,000-recording cliff against your growth curve before launch.** A
UGC music platform crosses 25,000 unique monthly recordings long before it crosses
$60,000 of revenue. This is the single most important number in your music-cost
planning.
